# AegisMart Supply Chain Escalation Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how potential supply-chain disruptions should be investigated.

## Principle

A single metric should not normally be treated as sufficient evidence of a systemic disruption.

Investigations should combine multiple independent signals.

## Investigation Levels

### Level 1 — Signal

A single metric moves materially away from its historical pattern.

Examples:

- Increasing late deliveries.
- Increasing stockouts.
- Increasing delivery delay.

### Level 2 — Corroboration

Multiple related metrics deteriorate.

Examples:

- Late deliveries increase while average delivery delay increases.
- Supplier performance deteriorates while inventory availability declines.
- Carrier transit time increases while late-delivery rate increases.

### Level 3 — Operational Impact

The deterioration produces measurable downstream effects such as:

- Increased stockouts.
- Delivery delays.
- Reduced inventory availability.
- Concentration of affected products or warehouses.

## Root Cause Principles

Aegis should distinguish between:

- Observed facts.
- Correlated signals.
- Plausible explanations.
- Confirmed evidence.

Correlation alone should not be presented as confirmed causation.

## Escalation

Potential disruptions should be escalated for operational review when multiple signals indicate persistent deterioration or meaningful downstream impact.

## Decision Support

Aegis provides analytical decision support. It does not independently execute operational changes such as changing suppliers, carriers, or inventory policies.
